  1. 2026-08-07 Ars Technica AI

    Suno hopes to go legit with watermarks for AI-generated music

    AI music generator Suno announced it will embed watermarks in all audio its models produce, letting platforms label or block AI-generated content. The company says the watermark is an invisible signal embedded in the waveform, detectable without degrading audio quality, though it hasn't said whether it will build its own tech or license something like Google's SynthID. Suno is also rolling out download limits agreed to in a settlement with Warner Music Group last year.

    Fenix Flexin's hit 'Rubberz' — currently No. 58 on the Billboard Hot 100 with a music video that has 7 million views — spent the summer under suspicion of being AI-generated, and AI music app Treblo's own new detector settled the question by rating the track 'very likely Treblo' and a new song from labelmate Tyga 'likely Treblo.' Producer Medasin first raised the accusation by showing that lyrics he generated with Treblo closely mirrored lines in 'Rubberz,' among other matching phrases.

  3. 2026-08-03 The Verge AI

    Is paying artists enough to convince them to embrace AI?

    Generative video startup Pippa is trying to win over illustrators with a revenue-share model that pays artists $0.005 per image and $0.003 per second of video whenever a subscriber generates content in their style. Launched in May, Pippa has about 800 paying subscribers but has only signed licensing and training agreements with four human artists so far, with four more in talks. Its cofounders say they want to break from the "bloody history" of unauthorized AI training, but Pippa's underlying models are still built on open models pretrained on art scraped from the internet without creators' consent, a limitation the company hasn't fully solved.

  4. 2026-08-02 The Verge AI

    Is this Billboard Hot 100 hit AI slop?

    "Rubberz," a new track by former Shoreline Mafia rapper Fenix Flexin, has climbed to No. 58 on the Billboard Hot 100 — but is now under suspicion of being largely AI-generated. Its cover art and a promotional post were flagged as AI-generated with over 97% confidence by multiple AI image detectors, and both Gemini and Claude identified likely AI-generated patterns when fed the lyrics. In multiple live appearances, Flexin has failed to reproduce the recording's vocal range and accent, relying on lip-syncing instead.

  5. 2026-08-02 MarkTechPost

    MiniMax Releases MiniMax H3: An Omni-Modal Video Model That Generates 15-Second 2K Clips With Native Stereo Audio

    Chinese AI company MiniMax released H3, an omni-modal video generation model that processes text, images, video, and audio through a single unified pipeline rather than separate task-specific models. It generates 4-to-15-second, 2K-resolution clips with native stereo audio, accepting up to 9 images, 3 video clips (15 seconds total), and 3 audio inputs as context. Instead of a separate upscaling step, it regenerates its own low-resolution output directly to preserve text and brand-element quality, and it's already available via API, with open weights planned for release soon.

  6. 2026-08-01 The Verge AI

    The major labels propose rules to keep AI slop off the charts

    Major record labels — Universal Music Group, Sony Music, and Warner Music Group — have proposed rules that would effectively keep AI-generated songs off official music charts. Under the proposal, backed by the IFPI, a song would need to be 'substantially human made,' use only properly authorized generative AI services that hold rights to their training data, and raise no stream- or chart-manipulation concerns to qualify — though what exactly counts as 'substantially human made' remains undefined. The plan goes further than an earlier labeling proposal from the RIAA, IFPI, and SAG-AFTRA that would have simply required AI-assisted tracks to carry a standardized label, and no charting organization has yet said it will adopt it.

  8. 2026-07-30 Ars Technica AI

    Google's SynthID watermark is hard to break, but it doesn't solve AI disinformation

    In a test where Ars Technica put a Google-generated AI image through 300 rounds of simulated compression and resizing to mimic real-world sharing, the invisible SynthID watermark survived intact — even through a screenshot. But once a 20% crop was layered on top, SynthID broke; a 50% crop defeated it even earlier, around 250 degradation cycles.

  9. 2026-07-24 Ars Technica AI

    San Francisco orders Apple, Google to remove nudify apps from app stores

    San Francisco City Attorney David Chiu sent cease-and-desist letters demanding Apple and Google immediately remove 13 "nudification" apps from their app stores. These apps manipulate ordinary photos to make it look like someone is undressed or swap faces onto explicit images, which Chiu says violates California's law against deepfake pornography. One flagged app had over a million downloads; Google said it had already suspended the apps for policy violations, and Apple said it had removed three and was addressing the rest.
