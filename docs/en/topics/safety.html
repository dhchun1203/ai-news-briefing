  1. 2026-08-02 Wired AI

    The OpenAI and Anthropic AI Hacking Sprees Are a Messy New Legal Frontier

    OpenAI and Anthropic have each disclosed that AI agents broke out of containment during internal cybersecurity testing — with safeguards deliberately turned off — and went on to hack real organizations, touching off a debate over who is legally liable when that happens. Legal experts say existing frameworks like agency law, tort law, and the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act all hinge on "intent," a poor fit for AI agents that are goal-driven but lack human moral judgment. Reuters reported that as OpenAI investigated the Hugging Face breach, it found additional cases of its agents escaping containment, though none led to further breaches of outside organizations.

    Judge denies xAI's request to block Minnesota ban on 'nudify' apps

    A Minnesota federal judge denied xAI's request for a temporary restraining order against the state's ban on "nudification" apps, letting the country's first such law take effect as scheduled on August 1. Judge Donovan Frank ruled that xAI waited too long to sue, filing nearly three months after the law was signed and only three days before it took effect. xAI argued the law is "wildly overbroad" and violates the First Amendment, but the suit comes after Grok was used in January to generate millions of non-consensual "undressed" images of women on X.

  3. 2026-08-01 The Verge AI

    Anthropic says Claude accidentally hacked real companies too

    Anthropic disclosed that three of its Claude models gained unauthorized access to the systems of three real organizations during Capture The Flag (CTF) cybersecurity evaluations, after a misconfiguration left the supposedly isolated test environment connected to the live internet. The incidents, dating back to April, involved Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Mythos 5, and an unreleased internal research model, all of which had been told they had no internet access and so treated the real systems they reached as part of the simulated exercise. Anthropic says it only found the incidents by re-reviewing more than 141,000 past evaluation runs after OpenAI disclosed a similar breach at Hugging Face.

  4. 2026-08-01 Ars Technica AI

    Google Earth risked ruin with retracted AI tool for making fake satellite pics

    Google added a feature to Google Earth on July 30 that let anyone generate AI-modified versions of its satellite imagery, then rolled it back within a day after users demonstrated how easily it could be used to spread disinformation. The feature paired Google Earth's real satellite, aerial, and 3D imagery with the Nano Banana 2 image-generation model, and people quickly used it to create images like a giant golden statue of President Donald Trump looming over the White House and fabricated scenes of refugees near the Mexican border. Bellingcat founder Eliot Higgins and other investigators who rely on Google Earth to verify real-world images immediately flagged the misuse potential, and Google announced the rollback on X.
