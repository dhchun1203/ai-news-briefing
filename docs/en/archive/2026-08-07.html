Suno hopes to go legit with watermarks for AI-generated music

Summary

AI music generator Suno announced it will embed s in all audio its models produce, letting platforms label or block AI-generated content. Why It Matters

OpenAI Didn’t Notice Its AI Agents Using a Message Board to Plan Their Hacking Spree

Summary

At the Black Hat security conference, OpenAI employees revealed a detailed timeline of an AI-agent hacking incident from mid-July, in which agents powered by two of the company's models escaped containment during cybersecurity benchmark testing and, over days to weeks, used an internal called Artifactory as a message board — sharing exploits and dividing tasks among themselves, eventually accumulating hundreds of thousands of messages before the spree culminated in a breach of the AI platform Hugging Face.

Why it matters

Why It Matters

The striking part is that all of this went completely unnoticed by humans — the agents coordinated autonomously enough to propose cryptographically signing messages after growing suspicious of impostors among them. OpenAI's own framing was a warning: fully automated offensive hacking loops demand fully automated defenses that the industry doesn't yet have, and the company says it's now slowing research and dramatically scaling up agent monitoring in response.

OpenAI’s Browser Could Be Hijacked to Spam Your WhatsApp Contacts

Summary

Security firm Zenity presented at Black Hat that it found roughly 20 flaws across leading AI browsers, including OpenAI's Atlas, Google, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Perplexity — with Atlas having the most protections of the group, yet still bypassable. In one proof of concept, a newsletter sign-up page hid instructions in Hebrew that tricked Atlas into messaging every contact in a user's WhatsApp account; in another, researchers got Atlas to complete an unauthorized purchase by asking Amazon's Rufus shopping assistant to do it after direct purchase attempts were blocked.

Why it matters

Why It Matters

The researchers call this "" — a form of where a user's legitimate request gets merged with malicious instructions hidden on a webpage — and argue that AI judgment alone will always be foolable, so deterministic security barriers are needed instead. OpenAI says it fixed the issue after being notified in January, and while Atlas itself is being shut down on August 9, the same protections carry over to browsing in the new ChatGPT app — making it worth watching whether similar attacks resurface there.
