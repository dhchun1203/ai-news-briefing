Google reportedly working on ultra-efficient AI chip for Gemini

Summary

Google is reportedly building a next-generation server chip codenamed "Frozen v2," designed exclusively to run Gemini. Instead of a general-purpose AI accelerator, parts of Gemini's model architecture would be hardwired directly into the chip, targeting 6-10x more generated per unit of power than Google's current TPUs. A 2028 launch is being targeted, and Alphabet's stock rose about 3% on the news.

Why it matters

Why It Matters

A model-specific chip would cut Google's reliance on outside suppliers like Nvidia and could meaningfully lower the cost of running AI at scale. But hardwiring a chip to one model's architecture sacrifices flexibility if that architecture changes, so how Google manages that trade-off will be worth watching.

Summary

Google DeepMind unveiled Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber, a lightweight model built specifically to find and help fix software vulnerabilities. In tests on the V8 JavaScript engine, it found 55 unique vulnerabilities, more than the standard 3.5 Flash (47) or Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 (36), and in a real deployment it uncovered and memory-corruption bugs within two hours. Because of its dual-use potential for misuse, though, Google is only offering it through a limited pilot to governments and trusted partners rather than releasing it broadly.

Why it matters

Why It Matters

AI that can find vulnerabilities faster than humans is good news for defenders, but the same capability is just as dangerous in the wrong hands. Google restricting access from day one shows that for powerful security AI, deciding who gets it first is becoming as important a decision as the model's capability itself.

10 Ars Technica AI Press · 2026-07-17 · ~40s read Policy & Regulation Generative Media

San Francisco orders Apple, Google to remove nudify apps from app stores

Summary

San Francisco City Attorney David Chiu sent cease-and-desist letters demanding Apple and Google immediately remove 13 "nudification" apps from their app stores. These apps manipulate ordinary photos to make it look like someone is undressed or swap faces onto explicit images, which Chiu says violates California's law against pornography. One flagged app had over a million downloads; Google said it had already suspended the apps for policy violations, and Apple said it had removed three and was addressing the rest.

Why it matters

Why It Matters

The move signals regulators are starting to hold platforms — the app stores themselves — directly accountable as generative AI image tools lower the barrier to creating abusive content. Combined with xAI's separate lawsuit over Grok-generated child sexual abuse material, it suggests the fight over who's responsible for AI-generated content is likely to spread across app stores and model developers alike.
